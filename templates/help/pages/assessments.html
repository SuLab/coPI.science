Assessments

An assessment is BlackbirdBot's stored verdict on one screening interview with a lab. The Assessments page lists them, each assessment has a detail page, and both are where you record your own review. For how assessments come about, see How Blackbird works. Unfamiliar terms are in the Glossary.

The Assessments list

The page is titled Opportunity Assessments. It opens on the current simulation run, sorted for triage, on the Unreviewed tab.

{{ figure(request, "assessments-list", "The Assessments list: the filters at the top right, the review tabs, and the summary cards.") }}

Filters

The controls at the top right apply when you press Apply:

  • Run: which simulation run to show. Each run is listed by its start time and status, with how many assessments it has stored and, where recorded, the rubric version it used. The newest run is marked "current". All Runs shows every run on record.
  • Sort: Score (triage), the default, puts the highest weighted score first. Most recent, Recommendation (advance first, then conditional, route-to-incubation, and pass under the rubric's label) and Lab are the others.
  • Assignment: All, or Assigned to me for only the assessments assigned to you. A manager can assign an assessment to a reviewer. An assignment does not limit what you can see: every reviewer can open and review every assessment.
  • Lab: a single lab, or All labs. The menu lists the labs that have assessments under the run and assignment you picked.

The review tabs

Unreviewed, Reviewed and All split the list by whether anyone has written feedback. "Reviewed" means at least one person has submitted feedback on the assessment. Being assigned does not make an assessment reviewed, and neither does an approval status. Each tab shows its count, and the counts follow the selected run, lab and assignment.

The header

The line under the title says which rubric version the page uses. It also prints the score bands: the weighted score needed for advance and for conditional, and the rubric's label for scores below that. The weighted score is computed from the dimension scores and the rubric's weights. It is not taken from the model. When more assessments match than the page can display, a note under the header says how many are shown out of how many.

Warning banners

  • An amber banner counts lost verdicts. These are interviews whose verdict was never stored, or never produced, with each reason listed separately. A lost verdict cannot be recovered.
  • An orange banner counts verdicts whose specialist panel is incomplete, unverified or not recorded. Treat those scores as provisional. Each card's panel badge says which case it is.

Both banners count the whole run, across every lab and assignment, even when you have picked one lab or Assigned to me. So the assessment a banner counts may not be in the list below it.

Summary cards and dimension distribution

The summary cards (Total, Advance, Conditional, Route-to-incubation and Decline) count the assessments shown on the page by the hub's recommendation, not by score band. Dimension distribution opens a table of each rubric dimension's weight, specialist, count, mean, minimum and maximum, plus the number of assessments in each band. It covers the tab you are on. Its band counts use the stored name pass for the lowest band, which the rest of the page shows under the rubric's label.

The gating legend above the banners explains the met, not met and unconfirmed symbols used inside each card.

Reading an assessment card

Each assessment in the list is a card.

{{ figure(request, "assessment-card", "One assessment card, with the recommendation chip at the top right and Quick scoring at the bottom.") }}
  • Headline: the hub's one-line summary. On older assessments that have none, the project label is shown instead. The hub's confidence follows in square brackets. Under it are the lab (a link to the PI's page when Blackbird knows it), the date and the rubric version that scored it. With All Runs selected, the run is shown too.
  • Recommendation chip (top right): the hub's own recommendation: advance, conditional or route-to-incubation. A recommendation to pass is shown with the rubric's own label for it, the same word that ends the header's band line.
  • In one minute: the hub's short pitch for the proposal. At its foot (or on a row of their own when there is no pitch) are detail →, which opens the detail page, and chat, which opens the detail page with the assessment chat already open.
  • Key points: the hub's summary bullets, grouped under its own headings.
  • Why this score (closed until you click it): the weighted score, its band, and the hub's own explanation of the score where it recorded one.
  • Footer badges: the number of red flags (or "no flags"), the panel badge, an Approved or Disapproved chip when the assessment has an approval status, and who it is Assigned to and Reviewed by. The panel badge reads "panel incomplete", "panel unverified", "panel not recorded" or "panel state unknown". There is no badge when the panel found no gap or was not required.
  • Rubric scores & gating (closed until you click it): each dimension's score as a bar, with its weight, followed by each gate's state. A gate shows the hub's reason for its state, or the rubric's definition of the gate where the hub gave none.
  • Quick scoring: a short form for an overall rating, a comment and a feedback mode, without opening the detail page. It rates the proposal, not the bot. It has no per-dimension scores and cannot edit earlier feedback; use the detail page for both. See Recording your review.

Some older assessments have no headline, pitch, key points or score explanation. Those parts are simply left out of their cards.

The assessment detail page

Open an assessment with detail → on its card. ← Opportunity Assessments at the top takes you back to the list with your filters kept.

Jump links and Expand all

The bar at the top stays in view as you scroll. Its links jump to Brief, Evidence, Ask, Rationale, Panel, Gating, Scores, Review and Timeline. Ask and Rationale appear only when the assessment has them. Expand all and Collapse all open or close every collapsible section at once.

{{ figure(request, "detail-evidence", "The detail page's Evidence summary, with its Strengths, Risks and Not established groups.") }}

The sections, in page order

Brief
The headline and confidence, In one minute, Key points and Why this score. When staff have confirmed companies for the PI, the brief lists them as they stand today, with the date.
Evidence summary
Strengths, Risks and Not established, plus Mid-scale for dimensions that are neither a strength nor a risk. Each is derived from the stored verdict's dimension scores, gates, red flags and specialist consults, and grouped by those sources. A row may carry the hub's one-sentence reason ("Hub's reason:") or, for a gate, the rubric's definition ("Rubric definition:"). "show" opens a row's quoted text.
The ask
The hub's recommended next experiment. On newer assessments, Show the full plan opens the plan behind it.
Verdict
The card that names the lab, which hub screened it, the date and the Slack channel. It shows the Hub recommendation and the Score and band. The score is computed, not taken from the model, and the card prints the band lines of the rubric revision that scored it. It also says which rubric version scored this assessment, and warns when that differs from the current rubric, because scores from different revisions are not directly comparable.
Specialist panel
Always open. It reads Specialist panel incomplete (naming the domains never consulted), Specialist panel unverified, Specialist panel: not required, Specialist panel: no gap recorded or Specialist panel: not recorded. Each consulted domain gets a chip with its signals. No gap recorded is only about coverage: every specialist the verdict needed was consulted. It says nothing about what they found: a consulted specialist can still signal gap or blocking, as the domain chips below the banner show. See the specialist panel.
Gating criteria
Closed until you open it. Each gate shows met, not met or unconfirmed, with the rubric's definition where available and the hub's reason where recorded. "Not met" means the question was asked and failed; "unconfirmed" means it was never asked.
Red flags
Always open, with a count, or "None recorded."
Full rationale
The hub's full written reasoning.
Dimension scores
Each rubric dimension as a bar, with its weight and the hub's reason for the score where it gave one. An unscored dimension shows "not scored" and counted as zero in the weighted score.
Human review
Approval status, everyone's feedback, the form to add yours, and who is assigned. See Recording your review.
Interview timeline
Closed until you open it. The Slack thread the verdict came from, with the specialist consults placed in order among the messages. The message that carried the verdict is marked "carried this verdict". Long messages are cut short; Show full message shows the rest. If the thread's messages were not kept, the section says "Interview messages unavailable."; the verdict above is unaffected.

Printing

Use your browser's print command. Every collapsed section opens for printing and returns to how you left it afterwards. The chat and its buttons are left off the printout.

What only staff see

Reviewers' pages, and their assessment chat, leave these out entirely.

Asking the assessment chat

The assessment chat answers questions about one assessment, from that assessment's record: the verdict, the interview transcript, the specialist panel findings, the human reviews and the scoring rubric. It never reads the PI's profile. It opens in a panel on the right of the detail page, or full screen on a narrow screen.

{{ figure(request, "chat-drawer", "The assessment chat panel: a question, an answer and its Sources list.") }}

Opening it

  • the round speech-bubble button at the bottom right of the detail page (its tooltip reads "Ask about this assessment");
  • chat on a card in the list, which opens the detail page with the chat already open;
  • an Ask: button at the end of a page section. It opens the chat and sends that question straight away.

Asking

Type in the box at the bottom. Enter or Ask sends; Shift+Enter starts a new line. In an empty conversation the chat offers opening questions written for this assessment ("Questions about this assessment. A click sends one:"). An AI model writes them from the same record the chat answers from, and questions built from the page's own fields appear whenever no written set is ready. Clicking one sends it at once.

The bottom of the chat shows how long your question is against the limit, and how many of today's questions you have used ("X of Y questions used today"). Each question you send, typed or clicked, counts.

Sources and Show in page

Under an answer, Sources lists the parts of the record the answer cites. Show in page next to a source scrolls the detail page to it and highlights it, opening its section if needed.

If the hub updates the verdict while the interview is still running, the conversation marks the point with Verdict updated after this point. Earlier questions stay visible, but they are no longer part of the conversation the model sees.

Clearing and saving

Clear conversation deletes your whole conversation on this assessment, after you confirm. It cannot be undone, and it does not give back questions you have already used today. Other people's conversations are separate and not affected.

Conversations are saved for you, and administrators with database access can read them.

When the chat is not there

When the chat is switched off for the site, no chat buttons appear.{% if permissions.admin %} While an admin is viewing the site as someone else, the page shows "Chat unavailable while impersonating" instead.{% endif %} The chat's own error messages are listed in Troubleshooting.

Recording your review

Your review rates the proposal's own scientific and strategic quality, not how well the bot performed. Read the full rationale first: the brief at the top of the page is BlackbirdBot's own summary of it.

{{ figure(request, "review-form", "The Add feedback form in the Human review section of the detail page.") }}

Add feedback

The Add feedback form in the Human review section has these parts:

  • Overall rating, required: a whole number from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).
  • Rubric dimensions (optional): one score per rubric dimension, on the scale the form shows. Each shows its weight and what BlackbirdBot scored, and What the scale means here explains the scale for that dimension. Leave a dimension blank if you cannot judge it.
  • Comment (optional): up to 10,000 characters. Use it to explain your rating and to flag anything the assessment got wrong.
  • Feedback mode, required: Learn or Don't learn — log only.

Press Submit feedback. Each submission adds a new entry; it does not replace your earlier one. Quick scoring on a card is the same form without the dimension scores.

{% call careful() %}

Feedback is real review data. It is saved under your name, everyone who opens the assessment can read it, and only an admin can delete it. You can edit your own entries.

{% endcall %}

Learn, or Don't learn — log only

Learn marks your feedback as eligible for prompt suggestions, and nothing more: nothing runs when you submit. Don't learn — log only records the feedback and never sends it there. {% if permissions.staff %}Suggestions are generated only when someone presses Generate suggestions from current reviews on the Prompt Suggestions page; see Prompt suggestions.{% else %}A manager decides when suggestions are generated.{% endif %}

Editing and deleting

Each entry in the Feedback list shows who wrote it, the rating, the mode and the date. Edit appears only on your own entries. It reopens the same fields, including the dimension scores, and Save replaces them. An edited entry is marked "(edited)". Nobody can edit someone else's feedback, and nobody but an admin can delete feedback.

What happens after you submit

Once an assessment has any feedback, even a rating with no comment, it leaves the Unreviewed tab and appears under Reviewed. When you submit with Quick scoring from the Unreviewed tab, the page says "Moved to Reviewed." and takes you to the next card.

If an identical review is submitted again straight away (a double click, for example), it is not saved twice. The page says "That feedback was already recorded a moment ago; it was not saved twice." Other messages the form can show are in Troubleshooting.

Approval status

The Approval status line at the top of the Human review section shows the latest approve or disapprove decision recorded on the assessment: Approved or Disapproved, with who recorded it. When there is no decision, or the last one was cleared, it reads No approval decision. Status history lists every recorded decision with who made it and when. A card in the list shows the same Approved or Disapproved chip.

No page has a control to change the approval status: it shows past decisions only. It is separate from feedback, and it does not decide which review tab an assessment is on.

Assigning assessments

The Assigned list at the bottom of the Human review section shows who an assessment is assigned to; everyone can see it. Managers and admins also get an Assign to menu with Assign, and Remove next to each person assigned.

The menu lists every reviewer, manager and admin whose access is allowed. Assigning someone who is already assigned changes nothing.

{% call careful() %}

Assign adds the person to the assessment's Assigned list, which everyone can see, and to their Assigned to me filter. Remove undoes it.

{% endcall %} {% call note() %}

Assignments are workflow labels only. They never grant or limit access: every reviewer can open and review every assessment, assigned or not. An assignment drives the Assigned to me filter and the Assigned line on the card. Remove undoes an assignment.
