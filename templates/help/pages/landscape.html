Landscape

The Landscape page shows opportunities that Blackbird finds by reading research from Scripps Research labs: papers, preprints, grants, patents and technology listings. A pipeline of AI stages reads each new item, keeps the ones that describe a way to treat a human disease, groups related ideas, ranks them, and keeps only the strongest few, the cut. Ideas in the cut that belong together become one opportunity, and BlackbirdBot writes an assessment of each, in the same form as an interview's assessment: headline, key points, verdict, gates, scores and rationale.

Each week a new batch of opportunities is issued, the same for everyone. You read each card and record a quick decision: pursue or pass. Unfamiliar terms are in the Glossary.

Landscape opportunities are not interviews and not projects. No lab agent pitched them, and the labs behind them are not on Blackbird's PI roster, so their names do not link to PI pages. The assessments are written by AI: check them against the sources before relying on them.

The Landscape page

Open Workspace → Landscape. Admins, managers and reviewers can open it; PIs cannot.

This week's batch

The first section is This week's batch: the opportunities issued this week, never shown in an earlier batch. The cards follow the pipeline's ranking of the ideas, the strongest first, but no rank number is shown; a blind batch (below) is shuffled instead. A batch can be smaller than usual, or empty, when the pipeline has few new opportunities to issue. Above the cards are the bands, as on the Assessments page, and tiles counting the batch's cards by recommendation: Total, Advance, Conditional, Route-to-incubation and Decline. A card whose recommendation you cannot see yet counts only in Total.

Carried over

Below it come the cards from earlier batches that you have not decided on yet, grouped by batch, newest batch first. A card stays there until you record your decision, so nothing is lost if you miss a week. Your carried-over cards are your own: another person's decision never removes a card from your view.

  • A card marked changed since your decision is one you decided on whose assessment has since been rewritten, for example because a new paper joined the opportunity. Your decision still stands; read the new assessment, then confirm or change it.
  • A card that says it has no completed assessment is waiting for its assessment to be rewritten. Its page lists the last earlier assessment under History.
  • An opportunity the pipeline has retired leaves your carried-over cards. Its page stays readable as history.

Every reviewer and manager also sees the Earlier batches in full.

Blind batches

Some weeks the batch is blind. Its cards are shuffled rather than in the pipeline's order. Until you have recorded your decision on a blind card:

  • the card hides BlackbirdBot's recommendation, the score and band (there is no Why this score), and the counts of red flags and deal-breakers;
  • the card still shows the headline, the pitch, the cost line and the key points;
  • the opportunity's page and its chat hide the score, the band, the dimension scores and the rationales for them. They still show BlackbirdBot's recommendation.

Decide from the headline, the pitch, the cost line, the key points and the evidence on the opportunity's page; once you decide, the hidden parts appear. A blind card's place in the pipeline's ranking stays hidden until the next batch is issued, so that no card stands out.

A card

{{ figure(request, "landscape-card", "A Landscape card: the recommendation, In one minute, the cost line and key points, Why this score, the footer and the quick decision.") }}

Each card follows the cards of the Assessments page. It shows:

  • BlackbirdBot's headline, which opens the opportunity's page;
  • one structured line for each idea in the opportunity (what it acts on, the disease and the kind of medicine), then the labs behind it, by the person who leads each, and the date it was issued;
  • BlackbirdBot's recommendation, in colour (a pass shows under the rubric's word for it);
  • In one minute: the whole elevator pitch, with a detail → button to the opportunity's page;
  • beside it, under Key points: first, in bold, the cost line, the cost and time of the next step BlackbirdBot recommends, from The ask; then the key points, each risk marked Risk:;
  • Why this score, closed until you open it: the score and band, BlackbirdBot's account of why the opportunity scored as it did, and the note "The band is computed from the dimension scores, not taken from BlackbirdBot.";
  • at the foot, how many red flags BlackbirdBot recorded and how many deal-breakers the deeper analysis confirmed (confirmed deal-breaker); Assigned:, with who is assigned; and, once you have recorded your own decision, Decided by:, with everyone who has decided;
  • the Quick decision (see Deciding and reviewing).

A part with nothing to show is left out.

Compact cards

Above the cards, Cards: switches between Full, the cards described above and the default, and Compact, a shorter card offered for comparison. A compact card shows the headline and the structured lines; BlackbirdBot's recommendation with its confidence, and the score and band beside it in grey; the opening sentences of the pitch; the cost line; the labs, the date issued and the counts of red flags and confirmed deal-breakers; a detail → link; and the Quick decision on one row. It leaves out the key points and Why this score, which the opportunity's page still has. A blind card hides the same things in either style. Your choice stays when you change view or tab, open a card or record a decision.

Current and All assessed

Managers and admins also get two lists. Current holds every opportunity in the current cut, and All assessed every opportunity that has an assessment, issued or not. Both have the Unreviewed, Reviewed and All tabs, which here are yours alone: Unreviewed means you have no quick decision on the card, Reviewed that you have one. No tab and no count reflects anyone else's decisions.

Both lists open with the bands, the same count tiles and a sentence such as "… ideas kept of …, grouped into … opportunities.", saying how many ideas the latest cut kept and how many opportunities they make. "Counts cover the whole list, whatever the tab and lab." Above the cards:

  • Sort: Pipeline order (the default), Score or Recommendation. A card whose score or recommendation you cannot see yet sorts last.
  • Lab: All labs, or one lab; an opportunity with several labs is kept when any of them is the lab chosen.

Press Apply to show the list with your choices. They stay when you change tab, open a card or record a decision.

While you have undecided cards in a blind batch, both lists leave out every card of that batch, so the lists cannot show you what the blind cards hide.

Being re-judged

When Blackbird changes the criteria the pipeline judges by, the opportunities are judged again under the new criteria. Until that finishes, the page says "Being re-judged under new criteria" and keeps showing the last complete set of assessments.

An opportunity's page

{{ figure(request, "landscape-detail-brief", "The brief on an opportunity's page: the headline, In one minute, Why this score and the key points with their badges.") }} {{ figure(request, "landscape-detail-verdict", "The Verdict: BlackbirdBot's recommendation beside the score and band.") }} {{ figure(request, "landscape-detail-dealbreakers", "Deal-breaker flags: a raised flag the deep analysis confirmed, and the flags checked and not raised.") }}

The page is headed Opportunity detail, with BlackbirdBot's headline at the top of the brief. It is laid out like an assessment's page. The On this page bar jumps to each section (Brief, Evidence, Ask, Verdict, Gating, Rationale, Scores, Deal-breakers, Review, Ideas), and Expand all and Collapse all open or close every section at once.

The page shows the opportunity's current assessment. Earlier assessments of the same opportunity are listed under History; each row opens that assessment read-only, under the note "This is an earlier assessment of this opportunity, shown as history." That view shows the assessment's own sections only: no decision or review forms, no chat, no deal-breaker flags and no ideas. It is never shown as current.

The opportunity's sections, in page order

Brief
The headline, the structured lines, the elevator pitch and the key points, each risk marked Risk:, with Why this score beside the pitch. Each key point carries a badge saying what supports it:
  • sourced, in green: a document, a database record or a verified quote. Its sources are listed under the point, linked where they have a DOI, a PubMed id or a clinical-trial id; patents and database records are named but not linked.
  • analysis, in grey: a specialist's or the pipeline's own note, not a source.
  • estimate, in grey: BlackbirdBot's own judgement.
Where the text names one of the opportunity's documents, it shows that document's title, linked where it can be.
Evidence summary
What the assessment holds, sorted into strengths, risks and what is not established, as on an assessment, with the specialist panel's findings. A finding about one idea links to that idea's section below.
The ask
The next step BlackbirdBot recommends: its first line, in bold, gives the cost and time, and Show the full plan opens the rest. Beneath it, Budget specialist shows the budget specialist's view of each idea, so you can see when the panel disputed the cost.
Verdict
BlackbirdBot's recommendation, with its confidence, and the Score and band, which Blackbird computes from the dimension scores rather than taking from BlackbirdBot; the bands the score falls into are printed beside it. When the two disagree, a sentence says so. The section also says which set of judging criteria produced the assessment, and carries the rubric's note that these calls are provisional and advisory. When the criteria in use were let through by an admin after they failed a check, it says "These criteria went into use by an admin override after a failed check."
Gating criteria, Red flags, Full rationale and Dimension scores
As on an assessment: see The assessment detail page. Gating criteria starts closed; Full rationale (with its number of paragraphs) and Dimension scores start open. When the deeper analysis confirmed a deal-breaker but BlackbirdBot recorded no red flag, the red flags say so.
Deal-breaker flags
Each deal-breaker the pipeline raised or the deeper analysis looked at, with its evidence in words: confirmed (amber) when the analysis confirmed it, cleared (green) when it cleared it. The deal-breakers that were checked and not raised are named on one line, Checked and not raised:.
Human review
Your decision and review, and others' once you have decided (see Deciding and reviewing).

The ideas in it

Then comes The ideas in it: one section for each idea in the opportunity, numbered Idea 1, Idea 2 and so on, each headed with its structured line. The first is open and the rest are closed until you open them. Each has:

  • its type, stage and evidence grade, in words;
  • Target biology: what is known about the target and how it works;
  • Disease profile: the disease, the patients and what treats them today;
  • on each line of the target biology and each field of the disease profile, the same sourced, analysis or estimate badge as on the key points;
  • the specialist panel's opinions; a long one is cut short, and Show full message shows the rest;
  • Verified quotes: passages checked word for word against the full text or the summary of each source, grouped by section, or "No verified quotes for this idea.";
  • Sources: every document, with its kind (paper, preprint, grant, patent, technology listing or dataset), a link where it has a DOI or a PubMed id, its role basis (why it counts as the lab's own work), its licence and its text basis (whether the full text or only the summary was read);
  • Labs: the labs behind it, with links to each person's ORCID and OpenAlex records where these are known.

Asking about an opportunity

When an admin has switched the chat on, the page has the same chat drawer as an assessment. It answers questions from what the page shows, and it hides what the page hides: on a blind card you have not decided, it knows no score or band either, and until the next batch is issued it knows no blind card's place in the ranking. It shares the daily limits of the assessment and project chats, and the bottom of the drawer shows your usage. See Asking the assessment chat for how to use it.

Deciding and reviewing

The quick decision

Every card and every opportunity's page carries the Quick decision:

  • Pursue or Pass, required;
  • Reason tags (what drove your decision), optional, chosen from the list the form shows; on a card they are folded under Reason tags (optional);
  • a short reason in your own words, optional.

Record decision saves it. On a card, the page then returns to the list at that card, or at the next card when your decision moves it off the list you were reading. Each person has one decision per opportunity, and you can edit yours. A decision belongs to the opportunity, not to one assessment of it, so it carries over when the assessment is rewritten; the card then says changed since your decision until you confirm or change it.

Your decision comes first

Until you have recorded your own quick decision on an opportunity, you see nobody else's: no decisions, no reviews, no approval marks and no counts of them, on the card, on the page or in the chat. This holds for managers and admins too. A full review does not lift it; only the quick decision does. Once you have decided, everyone's decisions and reviews appear, the card shows Decided by:, and they stay visible when the assessment is rewritten.

The full review

Under Human review on the opportunity's page you can also write a full review, as for an assessment:

  • Overall rating (1 = weak … 5 = strong): a whole number from 1 to 5;
  • dimension scores, optional, on the rubric's dimensions. What the scale means here explains each dimension's scale, and BlackbirdBot scored … shows BlackbirdBot's own score for it, where you may see it;
  • a comment, up to 10,000 characters.

The page has no approve or disapprove buttons. A mark of approved or disapproved recorded earlier still shows, read-only.

Decisions and reviews are saved under your name, and everyone who has made their own decision on the opportunity can read them. You can edit your own; only an admin can delete a review.

Decisions, tags and reasons are recorded for a later version of the pipeline. Nothing learns from them now: they do not change the pipeline's judgements, and they never reach the prompt-suggestion bot.

Expert judgments

A manager can mark some accounts as experts. Now and then an expert is given a few opportunities of their own to judge in full, listed on their Landscape page. On those, the page hides the pipeline's own calls (gates, scores, band, recommendation, red flags, the panel's opinions and the rationales) until the expert submits, so the expert's judgement is their own. Until then the card shows no recommendation and no flag counts either, and an analysis badge names no source. The expert's form asks for gate calls, dimension scores, deal-breaker calls and a recommendation.

Assigning

Managers and admins can assign an opportunity to a person from its page and remove the assignment again. Assignments are workflow labels only: they grant no access and send no notification.

The pipeline page

{% if permissions.staff %}

Managers and admins open the pipeline from the Landscape page, or directly: the pipeline page. It shows how the pipeline is running:

  • the phase (pilot, backfill or continuous) and whether it is paused;
  • the sets of judging criteria: the one in use, and any new set being checked or held, with the results of its checks. A set an admin let go ahead after it was held reads Held, then overridden, with the date; the reasons for the hold and the override are folded under Reasons;
  • for each source, when it last fetched and, under Completeness (last slice), how complete its most recent intake slice is, not the whole source. A slice that received more than expected reads complete;
  • counts by stage and source, refusals, spend by day, the cost per useful opportunity, and how well quotes and names were checked;
  • the latest cut, in a sentence: "Latest cut: … ideas ranked, … kept, … opportunities", and when it was made;
  • the reports: the model comparison, the positive-control trace and the drift report;
  • incomplete intake slices;
  • the audit of rejected items, with every disagreement named by the item's title and reason, and the items each stage filtered out, with the reason.

Blind batches also carry a few opportunities from below the cut, to check that the ranking works. Which cards those were, and the blind comparison's result, appear here only after the next batch replaces the blind one.

Controls

Each control asks you to confirm before it acts.

  • Pause and Resume: pausing stops the pipeline from starting new work; work already sent finishes. Resume starts it again.
  • Rescue: puts an item a stage filtered out back into the pipeline at the next stage, with your reason. It then competes like any other item.
  • Positive controls: the papers behind Blackbird's past positive decisions, by DOI or PubMed id. The pipeline traces each one and reports where it was dropped.
  • Experts: mark or unmark an account as an expert.
{% call careful() %}

Pause and Resume act at once for everyone, and each undoes the other. A rescue is recorded with your name and reason and cannot be undone from the page. Changes to the positive controls and the experts take effect at once; you can change them back.

{% endcall %}

Only an admin can use Start backfill, Re-run bake-off and Override, which lets a held set of criteria go ahead.
