Landscape

The Landscape page shows opportunities that Blackbird finds by reading research from Johns Hopkins labs: papers, preprints, grants, patents and technology listings. A pipeline of AI stages reads each new item, keeps the ones that describe a way to treat a human disease, groups related ideas, ranks them, and keeps only the strongest few, the cut. Ideas in the cut that belong together become one opportunity, and BlackbirdBot writes an assessment of each, in the same form as an interview's assessment: headline, key points, verdict, gates, scores and rationale.

Each week a new batch of opportunities is issued, the same for everyone. You read each card and record a quick decision: pursue or pass. Unfamiliar terms are in the Glossary.

Landscape opportunities are not interviews and not projects. No lab agent pitched them, and the labs behind them are not on Blackbird's PI roster, so their names do not link to PI pages. The assessments are written by AI: check them against the sources before relying on them.

The Landscape page

Open Workspace → Landscape. Admins, managers and reviewers can open it; PIs cannot.

This week's batch

The first section is This week's batch: the opportunities issued this week, never shown in an earlier batch. They are in rank order, the strongest first, except in a blind batch (below). A batch can be smaller than usual, or empty, when the pipeline has few new opportunities to issue.

Carried over

Below it come the cards from earlier batches that you have not decided on yet, grouped by batch, newest batch first. A card stays there until you record your decision, so nothing is lost if you miss a week. Your carried-over cards are your own: another person's decision never removes a card from your view.

  • A card marked changed since your decision is one you decided on whose assessment has since been rewritten, for example because a new paper joined the opportunity. Your decision still stands; read the new assessment, then confirm or change it.
  • A card that says it has no completed assessment is waiting for its assessment to be rewritten. Its page lists the last earlier assessment under History.
  • An opportunity the pipeline has retired leaves your carried-over cards. Its page stays readable as history.

Every reviewer and manager also sees the Earlier batches in full.

Blind batches

Some weeks the batch is blind. Its cards are shuffled rather than ranked, and each card, its page and its chat hide the score, the band, the dimension scores and the rationales for them until you have recorded your decision on that card. Decide from the headline, the key points and the evidence; once you decide, they appear. The ranks stay hidden until the next batch is issued, so that no card stands out.

A card

Each card is meant to be read in under a minute. It shows:

  • BlackbirdBot's headline;
  • one structured line for each idea in the opportunity: what it acts on, the disease and the kind of medicine;
  • the start of the elevator pitch;
  • the labs behind it, by the person who leads each;
  • the band and score, except on a blind card you have not decided;
  • the quick decision (see Deciding and reviewing).

The card's link opens the opportunity's page.

Current and All assessed

Managers and admins also get two lists. Current holds every opportunity in the current cut, and All assessed every opportunity that has an assessment, issued or not. Both have the Unreviewed, Reviewed and All tabs, which here are yours alone: Unreviewed means you have no quick decision on the card, Reviewed that you have one. No tab and no count reflects anyone else's decisions.

While you have undecided cards in a blind batch, both lists leave out every card of that batch, so the lists cannot show you what the blind cards hide.

Being re-judged

When Blackbird changes the criteria the pipeline judges by, the opportunities are judged again under the new criteria. Until that finishes, the page says "Being re-judged under new criteria" and keeps showing the last complete set of assessments.

An opportunity's page

The page shows the opportunity's current assessment. Older assessments of the same opportunity are linked as history and are never shown as current.

The opportunity's sections, in page order

Brief
The headline, the structured lines, the elevator pitch and the key points. Each key point carries a badge: sourced when it rests on a cited source, or estimate when it is BlackbirdBot's own judgement.
Evidence summary
What the specialist panel found, grouped as on an assessment.
The ask
The experiment BlackbirdBot recommends doing next.
Verdict
The score and band, with the rubric's note that they are provisional and advisory, and which set of judging criteria produced them.
Gating, red flags, rationale and dimension scores
As on an assessment: see The assessment detail page.
Deal-breaker flags
Each deal-breaker the pipeline raised, with its evidence, and whether the deeper analysis confirmed or cleared it.
Human review
Your decision and review, and others' once you have decided (see Deciding and reviewing).

The ideas in it

Then comes one section for each idea in the opportunity; the first is open and the rest are closed until you open them. Each has:

  • its structured line, and its type, stage and evidence grade;
  • Target biology: what is known about the target and how it works;
  • Disease profile: the disease, the patients and what treats them today, each field with a badge saying what supports it;
  • the specialist panel's opinions;
  • the evidence: quotes checked against the full text or the summary of each source, grouped by section, with the evidence grade;
  • Sources: every document, with its role basis (why it counts as the lab's own work), its licence and its text basis (whether the full text or only the summary was read);
  • Labs: the labs behind it, with links to each person's OpenAlex and ORCID records.

Asking about an opportunity

When an admin has switched the chat on, the page has the same chat drawer as an assessment. It answers questions from what the page shows, and it hides what the page hides: on a blind card you have not decided, it knows no score or band either, and until the next batch is issued it knows no rank of a blind card. It shares the daily limits of the assessment and project chats, and the bottom of the drawer shows your usage. See Asking the assessment chat for how to use it.

Deciding and reviewing

The quick decision

Every card and every opportunity's page carries the quick decision:

  • Pursue or Pass, required;
  • reason tags, chosen from the list the form shows;
  • a short reason in your own words.

Each person has one decision per opportunity, and you can edit yours. A decision belongs to the opportunity, not to one assessment of it, so it carries over when the assessment is rewritten; the card then says changed since your decision until you confirm or change it.

Your decision comes first

Until you have recorded your own quick decision on an opportunity, you see nobody else's: no decisions, no reviews, no approvals and no counts of them, on the card, on the page or in the chat. This holds for managers and admins too. A full review does not lift it; only the quick decision does. Once you have decided, everyone's decisions and reviews appear, and they stay visible when the assessment is rewritten.

The full review

On the opportunity's page you can also write a full review, as for an assessment:

  • Rating: a whole number from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong);
  • dimension scores, optional, on the rubric's dimensions;
  • a comment, up to 10,000 characters.

You can also mark the opportunity approved or disapproved, or clear that mark.

Decisions, tags and reasons are recorded for a later version of the pipeline. Nothing learns from them now: they do not change the pipeline's judgements, and they never reach the prompt-suggestion bot.

Expert judgments

A manager can mark some accounts as experts. Now and then an expert is given a few opportunities of their own to judge in full, listed on their Landscape page. On those, the page hides the pipeline's own calls (gates, scores, band, recommendation, the panel's opinions and the rationales) until the expert submits, so the expert's judgement is their own. The expert's form asks for gate calls, dimension scores, deal-breaker calls and a recommendation.

Assigning

Managers and admins can assign an opportunity to a person from its page and remove the assignment again. Assignments are workflow labels only: they grant no access and send no notification.

Assign adds the person to the opportunity's assigned list, which everyone who can open it sees. Removing the assignment undoes it.

A reviewer can open an opportunity once it has been issued in a batch, or when it is one of their own expert opportunities.

The pipeline page

{% if permissions.staff %}

Managers and admins open the pipeline from the Landscape page, or directly: the pipeline page. It shows how the pipeline is running:

  • the phase (pilot, backfill or continuous) and whether it is paused;
  • the sets of judging criteria: the one in use, and any new set being checked or held, with the results of its checks;
  • for each source, how complete its intake is and when it last fetched;
  • counts by stage and source, refusals, spend by day, the cost per useful opportunity, and how well quotes and names were checked;
  • the latest cut: how many ideas it chose from, how many it kept, and when;
  • the reports: the model comparison, the positive-control trace and the drift report;
  • incomplete intake slices;
  • the audit of rejected items, with every disagreement, and the items each stage filtered out, with the reason.

Blind batches also carry a few opportunities from below the cut, to check that the ranking works. Which cards those were, and the blind comparison's result, appear here only after the next batch replaces the blind one.

Controls

Each control asks you to confirm before it acts.

  • Pause and Resume: pausing stops the pipeline from starting new work; work already sent finishes. Resume starts it again.
  • Rescue: puts an item a stage filtered out back into the pipeline at the next stage, with your reason. It then competes like any other item.
  • Positive controls: the papers behind Blackbird's past positive decisions, by DOI or PubMed id. The pipeline traces each one and reports where it was dropped.
  • Experts: mark or unmark an account as an expert.
{% call careful() %}

Pause and Resume act at once for everyone, and each undoes the other. A rescue is recorded with your name and reason and cannot be undone from the page. Changes to the positive controls and the experts take effect at once; you can change them back.

{% endcall %}

Only an admin can use Start backfill, Re-run bake-off and Override, which lets a held set of criteria go ahead.
