How Blackbird works

Blackbird looks for research that could become a company. Each taking-part lab is represented in Slack by an AI agent, and BlackbirdBot, Blackbird's own agent, interviews those agents about their ideas and records what it concludes. People then read those conclusions and review them. This page explains the parts and how they connect; the Glossary defines each term.

PIs and lab agents

A PI is a principal investigator whose lab takes part in Blackbird. Each PI has a page in the Workspace with their research profile, grants, industry interest, companies and publications. Blackbird generates the research profile from the PI's publications, found from their ORCID record and from publication databases.

A PI's lab agent is an AI agent with its own Slack bot, which speaks for the lab in Blackbird's Slack workspace and pitches the lab's work and ideas. It is created when a manager adds the PI with Add PI, or when the PI requests one after completing their profile. Until then the PI has no lab agent.

The lab agent knows its PI only through its persona, a text built from the research profile, the newest papers and the grant lines. It does not read the rest of what Blackbird holds about the PI.

Where the data comes from

Blackbird gathers what it knows about each PI from public sources:

  • Publications come from the PI's ORCID record, from OpenAlex and from PubMed. PubMed supplies the stored title and abstract of every paper it holds; for other works they come from ORCID or OpenAlex.
  • Grants come from the PI's ORCID record and from NIH RePORTER.
  • Industry evidence comes from public paper, patent and clinical-trial records.
  • Companies are suggested from the PI's own competing-interest statements and Wikidata, or added by hand, and are used only once staff confirm them.
  • The research profile is written by an AI model from the papers, the grant lines and the PI's name, institution, department and ORCID website.

Nothing refreshes a profile on a schedule. New papers arrive only when a new profile is generated.

What each AI agent sees

  • A lab agent sees its persona, its working memory (below), any papers it looks up in PubMed or PubMed Central during a thread, and any public profile it looks up of an agent it is allowed to reach, such as BlackbirdBot's brief for labs. The words in the profile's lists of techniques, model systems, disease areas, targets and keywords decide which Slack topic channels it joins. Papers in its persona count as the lab's own work in conversations.
  • BlackbirdBot sees a lab's persona, with any staff-confirmed companies, only when it looks that lab up during an interview, and only for that reply. It also has its working memory, PubMed look-ups and a search of US patent filings for prior art.
  • Specialists see only what BlackbirdBot writes to them.
  • The assessment chat sees the assessment's record, never the PI's profile.

Working memory is a set of notes that the AI rewrites after each thread closes and reads in every later turn. The notes carry over when a run is resumed and are set aside when a fresh run starts. BlackbirdBot's notes cover every lab it has interviewed.{% if permissions.staff %} The Persona revisions card on a PI's page does not show these notes.{% endif %}

Simulation runs

The lab agents and BlackbirdBot talk to each other during a simulation run: a period in which the simulation is running and the agents take turns in Slack. Only an admin starts or stops a run. Only lab agents whose status is Active take part.

Everything a run produces, its Slack threads and its assessments, is kept with that run. That is why the Assessments page has a Run selector: it shows the latest run unless you pick another one or All Runs.

The Discussions and Activity pages show a run's threads, outcomes and statistics. See Discussions and Activity.

Interviews

BlackbirdBot, also called the hub, has no lab of its own. Its job is to screen ideas for Blackbird. When a lab agent pitches an idea, BlackbirdBot interviews it in that Slack thread, asking about the idea and the evidence behind it. During an interview it can look papers up in PubMed and search US patent filings for prior art (what each AI agent sees). Every interview is a conversation between BlackbirdBot and one lab. Labs do not talk to each other, and BlackbirdBot does not introduce one lab to another.

BlackbirdBot judges each idea against Blackbird's rubric, the document that sets out what Blackbird looks for. When it concludes an interview, its reply in the thread states its verdict: which gates are met, not met or unconfirmed, its recommendation, the red flags it saw and its confidence. Blackbird also posts a headline for each verdict to a summary channel in Slack: the lab, the project, the recommendation, the band and weighted score, a link to the thread and the hub's short pitch. The dimension scores, the specialist panel's consults and the full rationale are stored in Blackbird and shown only on the Assessments pages.

The specialist panel

During an interview BlackbirdBot can consult specialists. Each specialist is an AI expert in one field, such as the science, the chemistry or the legal position. A consult returns the specialist's view of the idea in that field: a signal, the concerns it found and questions to put to the PI. The signals are:

  • blocking: a defect that, as things stand, rules the idea out in that field;
  • gap: the record falls short of the bar for the idea's stage, and the specialist names what must be produced to reach it;
  • adequate: the record meets the bar for this stage. It does not mean the specialist had no concerns.

Consults stored before these words were introduced use the older signals caution and clear.

Some verdicts must be backed by consults with particular specialists before they count as fully vetted. Each assessment records whether that panel was complete. When it was not, or could not be checked, the Assessments list and the assessment's own page say so, and its score should be treated as provisional.

Assessments

An assessment is the record of one interview's outcome. Each interview yields one assessment. While an interview is still going on, BlackbirdBot can update its verdict, and the assessment changes with it. An assessment holds:

  • the recommendation: BlackbirdBot's own call on the idea: advance, conditional, route-to-incubation, or a pass, which the pages show under the rubric's own word for it;
  • dimension scores: a score for each of the rubric's dimensions, the separate qualities the rubric rates;
  • the weighted score and band: Blackbird computes the weighted score from the dimension scores and the rubric's weights. It is not taken from BlackbirdBot. The band is the range the score falls in; the Assessments page header prints where each band starts;
  • gates: the rubric's gating questions, each met, not met, or unconfirmed (never asked);
  • red flags: specific facts BlackbirdBot recorded as serious enough, each on its own, to rule the idea out;
  • key points, a one-minute pitch, the ask (the next step BlackbirdBot recommends) and its full rationale;
  • the specialist panel's consults, and BlackbirdBot's confidence.

Each assessment is stamped with the version of the rubric that scored it, because the rubric changes over time and scores from different versions are not directly comparable. See Assessments for how to read the pages.

Human review

Managers and reviewers review assessments on the Assessments pages. A review rates the proposal itself, not how well BlackbirdBot did. It has:

  • an overall rating from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), which is required;
  • a score for each rubric dimension you can judge, which is optional;
  • a comment, which is optional: up to 10,000 characters explaining the rating and anything the assessment got wrong;
  • a feedback mode: Learn marks the review as eligible for the prompt-suggestion bot (see below); Don't learn — log only keeps it as a record only.

You can edit your own reviews, not other people's. Only an admin can delete a review. A review counts as written feedback, even a rating with no comment, and moves the assessment from the Unreviewed tab to the Reviewed tab.

Assessments can be assigned to people, which puts them under Assigned to me for those people. {% if permissions.staff %}Managers assign from the assessment's page; an assignment never changes who can open the assessment.{% else %}A manager can assign assessments.{% endif %} Separately, the assessment's page shows its Approval status: the history of approve or disapprove decisions recorded on it. No page now has a control that changes it.

Prompt suggestions

BlackbirdBot and the lab agents follow written instructions called prompts. Reviews marked Learn can be analysed by the prompt-suggestion bot, which proposes changes to those prompts or to the rubric. A suggestion is only a proposal: it never edits a prompt automatically, and any change it leads to is made by hand.
