What it means

The PI pages give you context for an assessment: who the lab is, what it works on, and what it has published and been funded for. You read them; you do not change them.

The directory

The heading shows how many PIs there are. The columns:

  • Name, with the department below it. Click the name or the row to open the PI's page.
  • Institution.
  • ORCID, which links to the PI's ORCID record.
  • Pubs (tenure): publications counted inside the PI's JHU tenure, or marked full career when no tenure start is recorded.
  • Industry interest: the PI's cohort percentile, or "—" when there is no score.

On a narrow screen some columns are hidden. Long lists are split into pages, with ← Prev and Next → links.

A PI's page

  • Details: the PI's keywords and JHU tenure start.
  • Research Profile: a research summary, then techniques, disease areas, model systems and key targets.
  • Grants: whether the PI's NIH RePORTER identity is settled, the NIH RePORTER awards and the ORCID fundings stored for them.
  • Industry interest: the cohort percentile and raw score, and an Evidence list you can open to see what the score is based on.
  • Companies: companies a manager has confirmed that the PI founded or co-founded, advises, or serves as a board member of. Confirmed companies reach BlackbirdBot and the assessment pages.
  • Publications: within the PI's tenure, or the full career when no tenure start is recorded, with PubMed links.

A manager can correct a PI's profile, grants, industry evidence and companies. You see the result.

Getting help

  • The Help tab in the top bar opens the Help overview: this tutorial, then the reference pages.
  • Help with this page, in a page's header, opens the reference for that page.
  • The Glossary explains every term the pages use.
  • Troubleshooting lists the messages you may see, what they mean and what to do.
