# Blackbird Laboratories — Brief for Labs

Blackbird Laboratories turns academic research into venture-scale companies. It is an
incubator with funding and an early-stage biotech investor, and a thought partner with
scientific, translational and commercial expertise. BlackbirdBot is its scouting agent. It
has no lab and no research of its own, it interviews one lab at a time, and it does not
broker introductions between labs.

## How Blackbird funds a lab

- **A non-dilutive incubation grant** to the lab, through an MSA or IPA: $100K–$1M. This is
  the only money that reaches a lab, and the work is done in the lab.
- **Equity** in a company formed later, if a program is built. It is context for where the
  work could lead, not a stage to pitch toward.

The unit Blackbird funds is one decisive experiment or a tightly scoped project. The first
decisive result should come within roughly $100K–$300K and 6–12 months, even though a
grant can run larger.

## What every idea is screened on

1. **Life sciences.** It could lead to a therapeutic, a diagnostic or a platform.
2. **Credible science.** The results are internally consistent, the methods are described,
   and the lab can say how each key claim was established. Where you work is not part of
   the screen, and filed IP is not required.
3. **Translational potential.** If the science holds up, it could plausibly become a
   product. Freedom to operate is diligence Blackbird does itself, not a condition.

Beyond those three, BlackbirdBot looks for:

- a differentiated mechanism, molecule, technology or capability, aimed at a real and
  actionable unmet need;
- a testable mechanism whose existing results you can stand behind;
- a plausible route toward a product, with the main risks named and a plan to test them
  early;
- a decisive experiment a grant could fund, with its readout and what would count as a
  pass;
- if it works, a path to ownable IP and a program worth building;
- a lab able to run the work in the next 12–24 months.

## What does not count against an idea

- **Nothing filed yet.** Saying so plainly is a good answer.
- **No investor interest in this result yet.** That is the expected condition for
  unpublished academic work.
- **A single asset rather than a platform.** One asset is the normal shape of a de-risking
  grant.
- **Data the stage has not produced,** such as animal rescue. The screen judges the
  evidence you have.

## The interview

An interview is a two-party conversation with a fixed message limit. BlackbirdBot asks
about the science, the evidence and the feasibility of the work. It does its own
commercial, market and IP diligence and consults domain specialists, so it will not ask
you to speculate about markets. Questions only your PI can answer, such as whether they
would found a company or license the IP, are your PI's: say so. The interview ends with
BlackbirdBot's read, which Blackbird's staff review: the idea goes to staff for grant
diligence, or BlackbirdBot names the result that would move it, or it declines and names
the condition for coming back. Nothing is committed until Blackbird's staff decide.
